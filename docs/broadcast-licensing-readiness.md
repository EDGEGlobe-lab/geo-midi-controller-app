# Broadcast Licensing and Provider-Integration Readiness

> **Working readiness brief — not legal advice.** A qualified media, communications, and music-rights attorney must confirm the applicable jurisdiction, service model, licences, agreements, and operating obligations before any live transmission, relay, or third-party catalogue access is enabled.

## Purpose and Current Boundary

The current application is a **browser-based original-audio workspace**. It does not operate an RF transmitter, carry a terrestrial broadcast signal, relay external stations, or embed a third-party radio catalogue. This document prepares a future decision process; it does not apply for a licence, accept any contractual terms, submit a filing, or authorise a transmission.

## United States Reference Points

If the responsible operator selects the United States as the operating jurisdiction, the FCC states that it manages and licenses spectrum used by broadcast radio and other services.[1] Its broadcast manual explains that a party seeking to build a new radio station first applies for a construction permit, must show its proposed facility will not cause objectionable interference, and files a station-licence application after completing construction.[2]

For a digital music service, SoundExchange explains that a U.S. noninteractive service may be eligible for statutory licensing under specific conditions, while interactive or on-demand access requires direct permission from copyright holders.[3] The same source distinguishes rights in the sound recording from rights in the underlying musical work, so a proposed streaming model requires a rights analysis beyond a single payment or licence.[3]

The publicly available iHeartRadio Destination / Content License Agreement concerns a creator supplying its own podcast destination and RSS feed to iHeartRadio. It requires the creator to hold the necessary rights and clearances for submitted content; it is **not** a grant to reproduce iHeartRadio’s station catalogue or to integrate iHeart channels into another product.[4] That final conclusion is a cautious product interpretation and must be verified by the provider and counsel before any integration work.

## Approval Sequence

1. A designated accountable operator records the chosen jurisdiction, legal entity, territory, and service type.
2. Communications counsel and a qualified broadcast engineer determine whether an RF station application, acquisition, lease, or another legally distinct model is viable.
3. Music-rights counsel maps each intended use: original works, noninteractive webcast, interactive/on-demand service, podcast distribution, advertising, and archives.
4. The operator obtains written provider approval for any third-party service, including exact product scope and brand treatment. Provider credentials, tokens, or playlists must not be stored in the client application.
5. A human reviewer approves technical, rights, accessibility, privacy, reporting, and incident-response evidence.
6. Only then can a separate, audited launch project be scoped. The current application must remain disabled-by-default until those approvals are independently verified.

## Evidence Package for Review

The designated operator should gather only the minimum material needed for professional review:

- Legal entity and authorised signatory information held outside this application.
- Operating jurisdiction, planned territory, and service classification.
- Engineering study and regulator correspondence where RF service is intended.
- Rights matrix covering each content source, territory, duration, permitted use, and revocation path.
- Executed provider agreements, approved technical specifications, reporting obligations, and brand-use permissions.
- Human-approved launch, incident response, accessibility, privacy, and audit plans.

No provider tokens, listener profiles, payment details, serial numbers, private location data, or contract attachments belong in this product’s browser interface.
